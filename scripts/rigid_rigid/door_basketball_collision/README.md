# door_basketball_collision

A hinged door swings open into a bedroom and strikes a basketball resting on the floor; the ball rolls into the room and the door is slowed by hinge friction. PyBullet solves the door leaf and the ball, and Blender renders the result. This is a rigid–rigid scene of PhyCoVE, the benchmark for physical counterfactual video editing (PCVE). The source video is 4 s at 24 fps, 96 frames, 1280×720.

## Scene

- **Inputs:** `assets/scenes/door_basketball_collision/scenario.json` holds the physics, layout and camera; `scene_config.py` loads, merges and validates it. `assets/scenes/door_basketball_collision/door_leaf.json` stores the measured door leaf (size, hinge, closing direction); simulator and renderer both read it, so nothing is measured at render time.
- **Door:** an 8 kg leaf, 2.0 m tall and 45 mm thick, in a 0.9 m deep jamb, hinged with limits at 0° (closed) and 105° (open). It starts 18° open, swinging into the room at 1.65 rad/s against a 2 N·m hinge friction torque. The leaf and its fitted frame are imported from `old_door.glb` by the shared `scripts/pcve_door_asset.py` (shared with home_basketball_door).
- **Ball:** a 0.62 kg basketball of radius 0.121 m, at rest in the door's path; the leaf reaches it around frame 5, about 33° open.
- **Room:** built by `appearance/room_shell.py` on the simulator's own planes. `layout.room_bounds` gives the west, east and north walls and the stretch of south wall left of the door; `assets/scenes/door_basketball_collision/door_leaf.json` gives the plane, opening and ends of the slanted door wall. On the straight walls the front face of the skirting board is the collision plane (a ball that rolls to the north wall stops right against the skirting) and the plaster is set back by one board thickness; on the door wall the plaster face is the collision plane and the skirting stands 35 mm proud, which is the simulator's skirt box. A corridor behind the door is generated by `build_hallway`; a framed picture from `cafe_wall_pictures.glb` hangs on its left wall as a scale reference.
- **Furniture:** a bed, chest of drawers, bookcase, books, potted plant, framed picture and an engineered-wood floor, all on the x > 0.2 m side, away from the ball's path (x < 0), so they neither hide nor touch the ball.
- **Lighting:** one window in the east wall. A sun lamp and a physical sky enter only through the window (with a portal); a garden panorama is shown outside, to camera rays only. There are no fill cards; the corridor lamp is 3 W. Emission of imported models is scaled by `layout.glb_emission_scale` and their roughness is floored by `layout.glb_roughness_min`.
- **Rendering:** Cycles with OIDN denoising, 8 bounces (6 transmission), a half-frame shutter for motion blur, and the colour grade from `layout.grade` (default AgX with the Punchy look, exposure −0.2). The camera comes from the scenario layout (`camera_lens_mm`).

## Physics

- **Solver:** PyBullet, 60 substeps per frame (dt = 1/1440 s), 150 solver iterations, gravity −9.81 m/s². Video frame i is the solver state at t = (i−1)/24 s.
- **Door:** a revolute leaf with hinge damping 0.02. Hinge friction is passive Coulomb friction applied every substep as a torque, capped at the impulse that would stop the leaf (`apply_hinge_friction`).
- **Ball:** restitution 0.58, friction 0.7, rolling friction 0.008, spinning friction 0.0004, linear damping 0.01, angular damping 0.02.
- **Room:** floor friction 0.6 and restitution 0.55; 0.2 m thick wall boxes with friction 0.5 and restitution 0.45.
- **Editable parameters:** `door_initial_velocity` (0–8 rad/s), `door_mass` (2–80 kg), `ball_mass` (0.2–12.4 kg), `ball_active`, and `timed_edits`, a schedule of `{frame, params}` events that writes `door_hinge_friction`, `door_mass`, `ball_mass` or `ball_active` into the running simulation at a frame between 2 and 96.

## Validation

- The builder rejects a source take whose door does not transfer motion to the ball, and an edit that is non-finite, indistinguishable from the source, keeps the ball after `DELETE`, or (for a whole-clip edit) shows no motion transfer. A timed edit must reproduce the source exactly before its start frame.
- The renderer checks all 96 rendered poses against the physical trajectory and the camera (`validate_render_geometry`) and writes `render_validation.json`.

## Cases

| Case | Edit (DSL) | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `source` | — | The door strikes the ball around frame 5; the ball travels about 1.7 m |
| `edit_fast_door` | `SET door.initial_velocity TIMES 2` | Initial angular velocity 1.65 → 3.3 rad/s: the door reaches the ball around frame 3, the ball travels 4.3 m, the door hits the 105° stop, rebounds and ends at 102° |
| `edit_heavy_door` | `SET door.mass TIMES 4` | Door mass 8 → 32 kg at the same angular velocity: the ball rolls 3.1 m and the door stops at the 105° limit |
| `edit_heavy_ball` | `SET ball.mass TIMES 6` | Ball mass 0.62 → 3.72 kg: the ball moves only 0.73 m and the door stops at about 37° |
| `edit_remove_ball` | `DELETE ball` | No ball in any frame; the door swings to 101° and is stopped by hinge friction |
| `edit_stiff_hinge_midway` | `SET door.hinge_friction TIMES 15 AT FRAME 3` | From frame 3 (t = 0.083 s, door at about 22°, before contact) the hinge friction rises from 2 to 30 N·m: the door stops at about 27.6° on frame 5 and the ball never moves; frames 1–2 are identical to the source |
| `edit_remove_door` | `DELETE door` | Hinged door removed from frame 1 |

`edit_vocab.py` defines the objects (`door`, `ball`), their properties and simulator bindings; the builder derives each edit's prompts with `pcve_edit_dsl.make_prompts`, and the source prompts are `DESCRIPTION` in `edit_vocab.py`. The builder names the source case `door_basketball_collision_baseline`; the edit IDs are the same as in the published suite (`--list-cases` prints them).

## Requirements

- A Python with `pybullet==3.2.7` (`requirements.txt`), for example `.venv-domino/` at the repository root. The builder solves the physics in its own interpreter; the renderer re-solves each case with `--physics-python`, else `$PCVE_PHYSICS_PYTHON`, else `.runtime/sim` (a link to `.venv-domino`).
- Blender 4.3.2 for rendering: `--blender` (or `$BLENDER_BIN`), otherwise the Blender found by `pcve_assets.blender` (`$BLENDER`, `blender` on `PATH`, `~/.local/opt/blender-*`); a Python with the `bpy` module also works through `--render-python`.
- CPU rendering is the default (`--device cpu`); `--device auto` prefers an OptiX GPU.

## Commands

Run from the repository root.

```bash
S=scripts/rigid_rigid/door_basketball_collision
export PCVE_PHYSICS_PYTHON=.venv-domino/bin/python
.venv-domino/bin/python $S/build_pcve_door_basketball_collision.py --list-cases
.venv-domino/bin/python $S/build_pcve_door_basketball_collision.py --out-root renders/door_basketball_collision/new_run --dry-run

# physics only, source and all edits
.venv-domino/bin/python $S/build_pcve_door_basketball_collision.py --out-root renders/door_basketball_collision/new_run --simulate-only

# keyframes of the source and one edit
.venv-domino/bin/python $S/build_pcve_door_basketball_collision.py --out-root renders/door_basketball_collision/new_run \
  --only edit_fast_door --mode keyframes --keyframes 1,5,24,96 --blender tools/blender-4.3.2-linux-x64/blender

# full 1280x720 videos of every case
.venv-domino/bin/python $S/build_pcve_door_basketball_collision.py --out-root renders/door_basketball_collision/new_run \
  --mode animation --resolution 1280 720 --samples 64 --device cpu --blender tools/blender-4.3.2-linux-x64/blender

# standalone solve of the source
.venv-domino/bin/python $S/simulate_door_basketball_collision.py --out renders/door_basketball_collision/sim/physics_transforms.json
```

- The builder always builds the source plus the edits named by `--only` (comma-separated edit IDs, all edits by default); it has no `--cases` option.
- `--mode` is `keyframes` (default; `--keyframes` takes comma-separated frame numbers), `preview`, `build` or `animation` (all 96 frames, writes `video.mp4`). The builder defaults are 960×540 and 24 samples; pass `--resolution 1280 720` for the benchmark format.
- `--skip-existing` reuses cases whose implementation signature and scenario still match.
- `tools/render_queue.py` (workflow `batch_render`) samples seeded door speeds and masses, simulates them and, unless `--simulate-only`, renders keyframes with `--render-python`.

## Outputs

Without `--out-root` the builder writes to `renders/door_basketball_collision/suite_build/pcve_door_basketball_collision_suite/`. Each case folder `<out-root>/cases/<case>/` holds `scenario_input.json`, `scenario_overrides.json`, a copy of the door leaf, `physics_transforms.json`, `simulation_metrics.json`, `prompts.json` and, after rendering, `cases/<case>/resolved_scenario.json`, `scenario_metadata.json`, `ground_truth_transforms.json`, `render_validation.json`, `door_basketball_collision.blend`, frames and `video.mp4`; the root holds `suite_manifest.json`. The published suite is `renders/pcve_suites/pcve_door_basketball_collision_suite/`; do not write new runs into it.

## Code layout

- `build_pcve_door_basketball_collision.py`: suite builder (cases, edit checks, render calls, manifest).
- `simulate_door_basketball_collision.py`: PyBullet solve of the hinged leaf and the ball, and the measured outcome.
- `render_door_basketball_collision.py`: Blender scene (door, ball, corridor, picture, camera), replay and render validation.
- `scene_config.py`: scenario loader, editable parameter ranges and timed-edit validation.
- `edit_vocab.py`: edit vocabulary, simulator bindings, edit list and source prompts.
- `appearance/room_shell.py`: bedroom walls, window, daylight and furniture placed from the simulator's planes.
- `tools/render_queue.py`: seeded parameter samples (workflow `batch_render`).
- `entrypoints.json`, `scene_manifest.json`, `requirements.txt`: entry-point description, scene manifest, Python dependency.

## Limitations

- The ball is a rigid sphere; inflation and shell deformation are not modelled.
- Hinge friction is a constant Coulomb torque; contact parameters are effective solver values, not measurements.
- The render sample count of the published videos is not recorded in the published suite.
- On some machines OptiX crashes Blender 4.3.2 with a segmentation fault; render on the CPU there.
